--- WHY THIS IS NOT A RE-RUN OF A FAILED MEASUREMENT --------------------------------------------------
ADR-188 tested a club's CLEAN-SHEET RATE. Over four gameweeks that is a five-valued statistic derived
from a coin flip (0,1,2,3,4 of 4), so its variance swamps whatever signal it carries. It returned a null
on the whole board, and a stronger null on DEF/GK alone (ADR-190's Option 3, spike 192).

xGC/90 is CONTINUOUS, minutes-normalised, and spreads 3x between best and worst club. It is a different
quantity, not the same question asked louder — which is the only thing that justifies a third attempt.

--- WHAT THIS DOES AND DOES NOT SHOW ------------------------------------------------------------------
DOES: there is a real association between how good a club's defence is and what its defenders score, and
clean-sheet rate was too noisy to see it. 4.2 SE is not a rounding error.

DOES NOT: prove the term ADDS anything. A defender at a good club already has a better points-per-90, so
part of +0.216 is information decision_xp already has — the exact double-count ADR-188 was designed
around with its delta-vs-league-mean shape. Whether the delta adds beyond the baseline is what a §B0
sweep must decide, and it must be scored on DEF/GK as well as the whole board.

ALSO UNRESOLVED: the confound. Good clubs have good players. This cannot separate "a solid defence earns
clean-sheet points" from "Arsenal's defenders are simply better footballers".

--- THE HONEST NOTE -----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
I measured this twice, concluded "no signal" twice, and told the owner his instinct was not explained by
the missing term. The measurements were sound for the quantity chosen. The quantity was badly chosen, on
the smallest sample the season could offer, and I treated a null from a noisy instrument as evidence of
absence. He kept saying "but Arsenal" and was right on each occasion.
